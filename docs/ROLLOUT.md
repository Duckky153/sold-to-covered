# Rollout plan: putting the checks on the floor

The plan behind the Adoption view. The point: a tool counts only when the floor uses it and the number moves.

## Before day one

- Work the activation queue by hand long enough to see the edge cases. Every check's "How to fix" card comes from that.
- Put the checklist inside the screen people already work from. Find out where each shift actually works (order view, email queue, portal) before launch.
- One number, agreed up front: **orders that moved to binding with a flag still open**. Second number: problems found after binding, per week.

## Week 1: pilot

- Two people on one shift. 15 minutes of training: walk through the How to fix cards, then the practice round (pass mark 9 of 10).
- Every flag ends one of four ways: fixed, dismissed with a reason, bind overridden with a reason, or moved open. All four are counted.
- Read every dismissal reason daily. Three of the same reason means the check is wrong, not the people.

## Week 1, later: the whole shift, then the next shift

- Add the rest of the shift once the pilot's open-flag number is near zero.
- Add the next shift. Watch its open-flag number on its own; a new shift is where adoption usually drops.
- If a shift skips flags, find out why on the floor before changing anything. In the simulated rollout the cause was where the checklist showed up, not the people.

## Week 2 and on

- Report the weekly number every week, before and after.
- Change a check when its dismissals say it's wrong, and note the change with its date.
- When the activation number holds, scope the next workflow (payments with pending cancellations).

## What is never automated

- Coverage advice, certificates and price changes stay with a licensed person.
- Nothing goes to a customer without a person sending it.
